# Processing and resource boundaries

Original captures retain their resolution. Capture/import/edit persistence admits at most 80 MP and 100 MiB of encoded image data; encoders stop at the byte boundary before a generated PNG can become unreadable by the app's own loaders. An oversized operation leaves existing managed revisions intact and asks for a smaller area/image.

OCR admits at most **16 MP per operation**, before full decoding or native pixel allocation. Use **OCR selected area** or crop larger captures. Recognition does not silently downscale text. Small-text enhancement retains its separate 8 MP bound. JPEG retains the 80 MP limit, WebP the 16 MP/16383-axis limits, and composition its existing 256 MiB estimate. Editor region operations use a 768 MiB working-image estimate; this is admission guidance, not a process-wide or native allocator ceiling.

Previews, rendering and export encoding share one background STA dispatcher. Native OCR uses its own lazy, reusable STA so a recognition pass cannot delay a newer copy, preview, editor operation or capture PNG encoding. Screen acquisition and selection cropping retain their separate reusable STA so an active encoder cannot delay the selection overlay. These three workers are bounded and started only when needed; the per-operation image budgets are not a combined process memory ceiling. Capture PNG encoding and clipboard preparation can still wait for other non-OCR image work. Native recognition/individual codec allocations cannot be forcibly interrupted. Canceled queued copies/previews/OCR do not decode, and active obsolete copies cannot publish. Capture admission covers acquisition, encoding, durable storage and clipboard delivery so slow storage cannot accumulate overlapping full capture pipelines.

Repository mutations resume on their caller's UI context. Pixels and immutable metadata snapshots are written on workers in serialized transactions; a replacement becomes visible only after its metadata commit. Active/pending revisions and file transfers retain leases, transfer grace remains durable, and failed metadata commits keep cleanup paused. Resume uses the yielding cleanup path. Individual filesystem metadata checks/deletes and settings persistence remain synchronous; this is not a claim that all filesystem calls are asynchronous.

History snapshot text uses one-time bindings; asynchronous thumbnail rows implement change notification and hold a weak window reference. Closing cancels/drains previews and detaches list bindings. Dock/history thumbnail caches and pending queues are bounded and decoded on the shared worker. Composition prepares/reuses thumbnails, and scrolling previews cancel superseded reads before replacing the display.

Regression coverage includes closed History-window collection, superseded copy preparation, bounded STA reuse and capture work during a blocked encoder, early OCR/encoded-output admission, session-name cleanup with pin/transfer protection, async revision rollback, concurrent storage and dispatcher responsiveness during injected slow writes. Multi-item pinning and batch export history each commit one metadata snapshot per operation; completed exports retain history on cancellation, and stale revisions or persistence failures report history warnings. Native probes and synthetic resource runs remain separate from physical capture, real clipboard/receiver, slow-storage hardware and two-hour/all-day acceptance. Q35 stays open. Detailed reports and temporary runners remain outside Git.
